# Nail Salon → Mobile App: Market Research

**Date:** 2026-11-14
**Purpose:** Inform a re-envisioning of the web game as a Google Play / iOS app, before any UI mocks or code.

Store data in this document comes from Appfigures (download/revenue figures are **estimates**, and some are
reported as bounded values such as "at most $5,000" — treat ratios as indicative, not precise). Review quotes
are verbatim from public store reviews. Web-sourced claims are linked at the end.

---

## 1. The short version

Five findings drive everything below.

1. **The category's defining failure is paywalling creativity.** The most common complaint across the
   biggest nail/kids-creative apps, in every language, is that free-form creation got moved behind a
   subscription. This is the opening.
2. **Henna has almost no app-store search demand — and almost no real competition.** `henna` scores
   **5/100 popularity** in the US App Store (the floor). But every henna app in both stores is a *passive
   image gallery*. Nobody ships interactive henna creation. So henna is a **differentiation, retention and
   press** asset, not an acquisition channel.
3. **Acquisition has to ride nail keywords.** `nail salon` (pop 49), `nail games` (48), `nails` (48),
   `girls nail salon` (32), `nail polish` (25), `nail art` (20).
4. **Generic nail-salon games monetize terribly** ($0.17–0.55 revenue per download). Curated, trusted,
   ad-free properties monetize **10–40× better**. The way to make money is to leave the genre's price
   expectations behind — be a premium craft toy, not another free nail game.
5. **"Simple vs Advanced" is the right instinct with the wrong framing.** Ship two *places*, not two
   difficulty levels.

**Strategic one-liner: name and acquire on nails; differentiate, retain and earn press on henna.**

**Structural notes:**

- **Toca Boca and Sago Mini have consolidated into "Piknik"** — one Spin Master subscription
  (~$11.99/mo, promoted at $9.99) covering Toca Boca, Sago Mini and Originator titles. The industry has

  reviews explain why.
- **Pazu is the volume player**, shipping dozens of near-identical "Girls _____ Salon" titles as a portfolio

- **Netflix licenses Toca properties** (Toca Boca Hair Salon 4 NETFLIX: 130k downloads/mo, no IAP) — a real
  alternative distribution path worth knowing about later.
- **Google Play categorises Hair Salon 4 as Education / Educational, not Games.** Worth copying: less
  competitive, better parent trust.
- **The US Kids top-grossing chart is dominated by subscriptions** — Toca Boca World, Lingokids,
  Reading.com, Bluey, Kiddopia, Keiki, Speech Blubs, **Pok Pok**, Hooked on Phonics, Buddy.ai. See §7 for
  why that does *not* straightforwardly mean "ship a subscription."

**The reference to study is Pok Pok** (#10 US Kids top-grossing): a premium, beautifully art-directed,
open-ended Montessori digital toy with no ads, no scores and no fail states. It is the closest existing
product to what this app could become, and it proves the model has room at the top of the chart.

## 3. The biggest opportunity: everyone paywalled creativity, and users are furious

Hello Kitty Nail Salon has accumulated **4,576 one- and two-star reviews** since Jan 2025. Reading them, the
theme is overwhelming and repeats in English, Spanish, Portuguese, Russian, Ukrainian and Hindi. A sample:

> "used to be so fun when I was a kid before all these stupid pay walls… **gives children no room for
> creativity at all**, the voice is so degrading when you don't do exactly what you're 'supposed' to do, can
> we get free freestyle back?????"

> "You can't do freestyle nails unless you pay for it??? who constantly wants to be copying a design they
> came up with and not do their own thing. **Especially kids?**"

> "antes el modo estilo libre era gratis ahora todo cuesta" — *free-style mode used to be free, now
> everything costs*

> "só da pra fazer 2 sem pagar" — *you can only do 2 without paying*

And the single most commercially useful review in the whole dataset:

> "what they mean it's a $10/mo subscription to use creative mode and more than 4 designs? …
> **I'd do a one-time fee to unlock creative mode and some basics. And even pay extra to add special
> designs. But subscribing is a no go.** Painting nails never occupied kids enough for that."

Toca Boca World and Avatar World (155,434 one/two-star reviews between them) show the same shape plus
reliability failures: purchases vanishing across devices, decorated houses resetting, content disappearing
after updates.

> "The free game is just enough game to draw a kid in, but just the right amount so that it also sucks for
> free… it is tricking little kids."

**Two things to take from this.**

1. The existing web app's promise — *no ads, no paywalls, no sign-ups, no internet needed* — is precisely
   what this market is failing to deliver. That is a positioning gift. It is also the thing you must not
   quietly abandon on the way to the App Store, because every review above is a user who felt betrayed by
   exactly that move.
2. **Never scold the player.** "The voice is so degrading when you don't do exactly what you're supposed to
   do" is a design note. Guided content must never grade, correct or fail anyone.

## 4. Henna: the real finding

**Demand (App Store / Google Play search):**

| Keyword | Market | Popularity | Competitiveness | Apps competing |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| `henna` | US, iOS | **5** (floor) | 56 | 246 |
| `mehndi` | India, Google Play | **5** (floor) | 86 | 250 |
| `henna design` | US, iOS | 5 | 54 | 249 |
| `nail salon` | US, iOS | **49** | 78 | 249 |
| `nail games` | US, iOS | **48** | 69 | 249 |

**Supply:** Every single henna/mehndi app found across ~186 results in both stores is a **passive design
gallery** — scraped photo collections wrapped in ads, named by year: *Mehndi Design 2024*, *Mehndi Design
2025*, *Mehndi Design 2026 — Offline*, *Latest Mehndi Designs 2016*, *Mehndi Designs 2016 10,000+*. Two
hundred and fifty apps in India fight over a keyword with floor-level search volume, and **not one of them
lets you actually apply henna.**

One notable data point: **Supercent** — a substantial hypercasual publisher — shipped an iOS app literally
called *Henna Design* in August 2023. Version 0.3.0. Never updated. No monetization. An abandoned test.

**What this means, plainly:**

- Henna will **not** bring you installs through search. Anyone promising otherwise is misreading the data.
- Henna **is** a genuine, defensible product differentiator — an interactive henna studio does not currently
  exist on either store, in any quality tier.
- Its value shows up as: App Store editorial interest, press and word-of-mouth, cultural reach into large
  underserved audiences, session length, and the reason someone keeps the app instead of deleting it.
- Demand exists but is quiet. One organic 5-star Avatar World review asks: *"could you pls add a house on the
  farm and **a henna salon**?"* Searching 10 major competitors' reviews for henna/mehndi returns 193 hits,
  but nearly all are the name "Hanna." Do not oversell latent demand to yourself — this is a market you
  would be **creating**, not capturing.

**The mechanic nobody has.** Real henna behaves in a way no app simulates: the paste goes on dark
green-black and sits in raised relief, it dries and flakes off, the stain underneath starts bright orange,
and it **deepens over 24–48 hours** into a rich reddish-brown before fading over a week or two. A design
that darkens overnight and fades over days is a genuinely novel, delightful, retention-driving mechanic —
and a legitimate reason to open the app tomorrow. Nobody is doing it.

## 5. Naming and ASO

### Why "Nail Salon" cannot be the name

`nail salon` has competitiveness **78** across 249 apps, and the top slots are held by Unickie, Pazu, Crazy
Labs, Budge and Lion Studios. You will not rank there on name alone, and the name itself is unbrandable,
untrademarkable and invisible.

### What the store actually gives you

- **Apple:** 30-char name + 30-char subtitle + 100-char keyword field (comma-separated, no spaces wasted).
- **Google Play:** 30-char title + 80-char short description; the long description is indexed too.

So: **a brandable name in the title, the keywords in the subtitle.**

### Recommended names

I checked each for conflicts in the games/kids space.

**1. Paisley** — *lead recommendation.*
The paisley (*boteh* / *ambi*) is the single most recognisable mehndi motif, *and* an everyday fashion word
in the West, *and* a warm given name. It bridges both halves of the product in one word, is pronounceable
everywhere, and is not held by any game or kids app (existing hits: a boutique, a cab firm, a chip shop,
Prince's Paisley Park).
- Apple: `Paisley` / subtitle `Nail & Henna Studio`
- Play: `Paisley: Nail & Henna Studio`

**2. Marigold** — *strong alternate.*
Marigolds are everywhere in South Asian weddings and mehndi nights. Warm, non-gendered, visually ownable
(instant palette), unclaimed in kids/games (existing hits: a health app, an icon pack, a garden tracker).
- `Marigold` / `Nail & Henna Studio`

**3. Henna House** — *safe, literal, lower ceiling.*
Alliterative and immediately legible, but it leads with the low-volume keyword and boxes the product in.

**Names to avoid:** anything containing **"Girls"**. It is the genre default (*Girls Nail Salon*, *Nail Salon
Games for Girls*, *Games for Girls Free*) and it does three bad things: caps the audience, ages badly, and
signals exactly the commodity tier you are trying to escape. Toca Boca, Sago Mini and Pok Pok are all
deliberately neutral invented words. That is not an accident.

> Trademark clearance is a lawyer's job, not a store search. Verify before committing.

### Keyword field starting point (Apple, US)

`nail,nails,manicure,polish,art,henna,mehndi,salon,kids,girls,design,sticker,toy,creative,pretend`

### Geography

- **Monetise in:** US, UK, Canada, Australia, Germany, Japan.
- **Henna resonance / volume:** India, Pakistan, UAE, Saudi Arabia, Malaysia, Indonesia, Bangladesh, Morocco,
  plus large diaspora populations in the UK, US and Canada. Expect reach and goodwill here, not revenue.

## 6. "Simple vs Advanced" — right instinct, better framing

Your diagnosis is correct: the current app is deliberately constrained for a 5-year-old, and that ceiling is
what makes it uninteresting to a 10-year-old, a teenager or an adult. But a **Simple / Advanced toggle** is a
known trap: nobody finds it, it splits your design and art effort in half, and it tells a small child they
are in the baby version.

**Ship two rooms, not two difficulty levels.**

| | **The Salon** (current audience) | **The Studio** (new audience) |
|---|---|---|
| Promise | "You can't get it wrong" | "You can make anything" |
| Nails | Tap a bottle, tap a nail | Per-nail layers, gradients, chrome/matte/glitter finishes, french tips, custom shape and length |
| Henna | Tap faded areas, they draw themselves on | Freehand cone with pressure/width, motif stamps, mirroring, repeat along a path, layers, undo history |
| Stickers | Big tap targets, auto-placed | Free place, scale, rotate, opacity |
| Canvas | Hand or foot | Hand, foot, both hands, arm, and a bare canvas |
| Errors | Impossible | Full undo/redo stack |

Choose the room at first launch by asking *who is playing* rather than *how good are you* — and let anyone
cross over freely from a persistent, non-condescending control. The Salon is the better onboarding for
*everyone*; the Studio is what stops the app being outgrown.

The Studio is also what justifies a price. "You can't get it wrong" is a lovely free app. "You can make
anything" is a product people pay for.

## 7. Monetization

There is a real tension in the data and it is worth stating honestly:

- **The Kids top-grossing chart says subscriptions win.**
- **The reviews say players despise subscriptions.**

Both are true, and the reconciliation is the recommendation. The subscription winners —
Lingokids, Reading.com, Kiddopia, Speech Blubs, Pok Pok, Hooked on Phonics — ship **continuously refreshed
content**: new lessons, new episodes, new activities. Parents accept recurring charges for recurring value.
The backlash is aimed at something different: apps that **retroactively paywalled a creative tool that used
to be free**, and now charge $10/month for a fixed feature set. That is what users correctly call a betrayal.

**Recommendation — premium one-time unlock.**

- Free download, genuinely generous: the whole Salon, a real set of colours, a real set of henna looks,
  saving and sharing. Not a demo. Not "2 designs then pay."
- **One purchase, once, forever** — around **$4.99–$7.99** — unlocks the Studio and the full library. Behind
  a parental gate, as Apple requires.
- **No ads. No third-party SDKs. No account. Works offline.** Say this on the store page in those words; it
  is your sharpest differentiator and the reviews prove people are looking for it.
- Optional later: paid design packs as **add-ons, never as a tax on what already worked**.

**When a subscription would be defensible:** only if you genuinely ship new content on a cadence. Henna
actually supports this honestly — it is an inherently occasion-based art form (Eid, Diwali, Karva Chauth,
Teej, weddings, Ramadan), which gives a real seasonal calendar. If you commit to a pack per occasion, a
low-priced subscription is legitimate. If you do not, do not pretend. Users can tell, and they write reviews.

## 8. What's wrong with the current app visually

Judged from `README-preview.png` — these are specific and fixable.

**The hand reads as a mitten.** Fingers are uniform rounded rectangles of near-identical width, the thumb is
a stub, and there are no knuckles, no taper toward the tip, no webbing between fingers and no wrist. Real
hands are asymmetric and tapered. This single fix would transform the app's perceived quality.

**Nails are decals, not nails.** Each is a flat oval with one radial highlight blob. There is no nail bed, no
cuticle, no lunula, no free edge, no thickness. A nail should sit *in* the finger with a soft shadow, not
float on top of it.

**The henna is clip-art, not mehndi.** Uniform 2px stroke, outline-only, evenly spaced, very low density.
Real mehndi has varied line weight, dense *jaali* (net) fill, dots of varying size, paisleys, and deliberate
density gradients — dense at the centre of the mandala, opening out toward the edges. It also has
**physical relief** (the paste sits raised on the skin) and **stain depth** (reddish-brown, not a flat
brown line). The current render is the weakest part of the app and it is also the thing you want to sell on.

**The palette has no anchor.** A lavender-to-pink gradient behind a mid-brown hand with pastel nails gives
nothing to contrast against. There is no deepest dark and no true white, so everything sits in the same
tonal middle and reads washed out.

**The composition is empty.** The hand floats in the centre with large dead margins, the cushion is a faint
ghost, and the bottles are tiny and cropped at the edge. The README promises "a little salon scene" — the
render doesn't deliver one.

**Direction for the rebuild:** warm, tactile, lit. Soft directional light with real contact shadows. Skin
with actual texture and subsurface warmth. Polish with specular highlight *and* reflected environment.
Henna with raised wet paste that dries, cracks and flakes to reveal stain. Think Pok Pok's material
richness, not flat vector.

## 9. Cultural authenticity and child safety

These are ethical obligations first. They are also, genuinely, competitive advantages.

**Henna is a living tradition**, not a decorative motif — across South Asia, the Middle East, North Africa
and their diasporas, it marks weddings, Eid, Diwali, Karva Chauth and coming-of-age, and carries associations
of protection, celebration and blessing. Regional styles differ substantially (Moroccan and Tunisian work
leans geometric and Islamic-influenced; Indian and Pakistani leans dense floral and paisley with fine
detail; Khaleeji leans bold, flowing and open). Building that vocabulary properly is what makes the app good
*and* what makes it respectful. **Commission a working henna artist to build the motif library.** Name them
in the credits. Authenticity here is both the right thing and the marketing.

**Do not render "black henna."** This matters. "Black henna" is natural henna adulterated with
paraphenylenediamine (PPD), often at concentrations up to 30% — far above regulated hair-dye levels. The FDA
prohibits applying PPD directly to skin. In one UK dermatology survey, **over 80% of patients presenting with
reactions were under 16**, and reactions range from eczema to blistering and permanent scarring, with
lifelong sensitisation possible after a single exposure. An app aimed at children that celebrates henna
should show only the natural reddish-brown stain, and should carry a short parent-facing note explaining the
difference. Offering a "black henna" colour would be actively irresponsible.

That note is also a trust signal most competitors would never think to include.

## 10. Compliance and risk

Verify all of this against primary sources (FTC COPPA rule 16 CFR 312, Apple App Review Guidelines 1.3 and
5.1.4, Google Play Families policy) before shipping — the secondary coverage conflicts in places.

- **COPPA:** the amended rule took effect 23 June 2025, with a compliance deadline of **22 April 2026**. It
  requires a written data-retention policy, a written security programme, and separate parental consent for
  certain third-party disclosures.
- **Apple Kids Category:** third-party analytics and advertising are heavily restricted. Purchases, external
  links and other distractions must sit behind a **parental gate** (which is *not* the same as verifiable
  parental consent). Once customers expect Kids Category compliance, you must keep meeting it even if you
  later deselect the category.
- **Google Play Families:** ad SDKs must be self-certified for child audiences and must disable personalised
  ads, interest-based advertising and remarketing. The policy covers **all** monetization in a child-directed
  app, including cross-promotion of your own titles.
- **The easy route:** no ads, no third-party SDKs, no accounts, no network calls. This sidesteps most of the
  above, is cheap for a local-first app that already works offline, and is a marketing asset.

**Other risks worth naming:**

- *Henna is not a growth engine.* If the business plan depends on henna search volume, it fails. Plan for
  nails to acquire and henna to differentiate.
- *Category gravity.* The nail-salon genre trains users to expect free-with-IAP. Escaping that price anchor
  requires the app to visibly not look like its neighbours — which makes the art direction a commercial
  decision, not a cosmetic one.
- *Two rooms is roughly double the design surface.* Scope the Studio carefully for v1; it is better to ship
  five excellent Studio tools than twenty mediocre ones.

## 11. Recommended next step

Mock, in order:

1. **The Studio henna canvas** — the hardest and most differentiating screen. If this isn't beautiful and
   obvious, nothing else matters.
2. **The Salon home** — the "you can't get it wrong" loop, rebuilt with the new art direction.
3. **The room chooser** — first launch, and the always-available switch.
4. **The nail close-up** — per-nail detail editing in both rooms.
5. **The parental gate / unlock screen** — the only place money is mentioned.
6. **Store listing mock** — icon, name, subtitle, first three screenshots. Worth doing early: it forces the
   positioning to be concrete.
